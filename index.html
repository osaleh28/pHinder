Intended use and sources

Intended use. pHormula is an educational and reference tool for licensed physicians and trainees. It calculates standard acid–base and oxygenation values and shows every formula and threshold it uses, so each result can be checked independently. It does not make a diagnosis, recommend treatment, or suggest ventilator settings; those decisions belong to the treating clinician. It is not intended for time-critical decisions or as a substitute for clinical judgment. It stores no patient data.

Rules and sources. Henderson–Hasselbalch equation for internal consistency (predicted pH within 0.05 of the reported pH). Winter's formula for metabolic acidosis (Albert, Dell & Winters, 1967). Expected PaCO₂ in metabolic alkalosis, 0.7 per mEq/L rise in HCO₃⁻. Respiratory compensation of 1 and 3.5 (acidosis) and 2 and 4 (alkalosis) mEq/L per 10 mmHg (Adrogué & Madias). Anion gap with albumin correction of 2.5 per g/dL (Figge et al., 1998). Delta ratio, delta gap and corrected bicarbonate. Osmolal gap, urine anion gap and urine chloride. Alveolar gas equation; A–a gradient norm of age ÷ 4 + 4. Berlin definition of ARDS (2012) and the 2023 global definition. Predicted body weight (ARDS Network, 2000). Brain Trauma Foundation guidelines (4th edition, 2016). Nasal cannula FiO₂ estimates are rules of thumb.
